Quadratic Equations

A quadratic equation is a second-degree equation with a graph shaped like a parabola.

Definition

A quadratic equation has the standard form ax2 + bx + c = 0, where a, b, and c are constants and a ≠ 0. The condition a ≠ 0 ensures that the highest power of x is 2.

Graphical Meaning

The graph of y = ax2 + bx + c is a parabola. Its real roots are the x-coordinates where the graph meets or touches the x-axis. If the discriminant b2 − 4ac is positive, the graph crosses the x-axis twice; if it is zero, it touches once; if it is negative, it does not meet the x-axis.

Relation Between Roots and Coefficients

For ax2 + bx + c = 0, if the roots are α and β, their sum is α + β = −b/a and their product is αβ = c/a.

Quadratic Formula

The solutions of ax2 + bx + c = 0 can be found with the quadratic formula:

x = (−b ± √(b2 − 4ac)) / 2a

The expression under the square root, b2 − 4ac, is called the discriminant.
